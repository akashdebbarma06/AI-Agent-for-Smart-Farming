# Organic Fertilisers and Soil Amendments Guide

> **Source:** KrishiMitra AI Knowledge Base — Agricultural Reference Document  
> **Domain:** Fertiliser Guidance

---

## Why Use Organic Fertilisers?

Chemical fertilisers supply nutrients quickly but can degrade soil structure over time. Organic fertilisers:
- Improve soil structure (tilth), water retention, and aeration
- Feed beneficial soil microorganisms
- Provide slow-release nutrition over weeks to months
- Reduce dependency on purchased inputs over time
- Are safer for groundwater and soil biology

Many successful farmers combine organic and chemical fertilisers (Integrated Nutrient Management) to get the best of both.

---

## 1. Farm Yard Manure (FYM)

- **What it is:** Composted mixture of cattle dung, urine, straw, and barn waste
- **Nutrient Content (approx.):** N: 0.5–1.5%, P: 0.2–0.8%, K: 0.5–1.8%
- **Application Rate:** 10–15 tonnes/ha for most crops
- **When to Apply:** 3–4 weeks before sowing; incorporate into top 15–20 cm of soil
- **Benefits:** Improves soil water retention in sandy soils; improves drainage in clayey soils
- **Tips:**
  - Use well-decomposed FYM only — fresh dung can burn roots and introduce pathogens
  - Well-composted FYM is dark, crumbly, and odour-free

---

## 2. Vermicompost

- **What it is:** Compost produced by earthworms (*Eisenia fetida* or *Lumbricus rubellus*) breaking down organic matter
- **Nutrient Content (approx.):** N: 1.5–3.0%, P: 1.0–2.5%, K: 0.5–1.5%
- **Additional Benefits:** Contains plant growth hormones, beneficial microbes, and humic acids
- **Application Rate:** 2–5 tonnes/ha; or 200–500 g per plant as root zone application
- **When to Apply:** At sowing time (basal) or as top dressing
- **Tips:**
  - Higher nutrient density than FYM — smaller quantities needed
  - Excellent for vegetables, nursery seedlings, and high-value crops
  - Can be made on-farm with agricultural waste; low investment

---

## 3. Green Manure

- **What it is:** Growing a fast-growing legume or nitrogen-fixing crop and ploughing it back into the soil while still green
- **Common Green Manure Crops:** Dhaincha (*Sesbania bispinosa*), Sunhemp (*Crotalaria juncea*), Cowpea, Cluster bean
- **Nitrogen Fixed:** 80–150 kg N/ha depending on biomass
- **When to Incorporate:** At 6–8 weeks of growth, just before flowering (maximum biomass and N content)
- **Benefits:**
  - Free nitrogen from the atmosphere
  - Improves soil organic matter
  - Suppresses weeds
  - Adds root biomass that improves soil structure
- **Best Use:** In the gap before the next main crop (e.g. grow dhaincha for 45 days before paddy transplanting)

---

## 4. Compost (Aerobic/Nadep Method)

- **What it is:** Aerobically decomposed mixture of crop residues, animal waste, and soil
- **Nadep Method:** Brick tank compost using 65–70% crop residue, 20% cattle dung, 10% soil
- **Decomposition Time:** 90–120 days
- **Nutrient Content (approx.):** N: 0.8–1.5%, P: 0.4–1.0%, K: 0.8–1.5%
- **Application Rate:** 5–10 tonnes/ha
- **Tips:**
  - Turn the heap every 15–20 days for faster decomposition
  - Compost is ready when dark brown, crumbly, and earthy-smelling

## 5. Biofertilisers

Biofertilisers are microbial preparations that help plants access nutrients from soil and atmosphere.

| Biofertiliser | Crop | Function |
|--------------|------|----------|
| *Rhizobium* | Legumes (soybean, groundnut, chickpea) | Fixes atmospheric N in root nodules |
| *Azospirillum* | Cereals (rice, wheat, maize) | Associates with roots; fixes N; produces growth hormones |
| *Azotobacter* | Cereals, vegetables | Free-living N-fixer; produces growth hormones |
| PSB (Phosphate Solubilising Bacteria) | All crops | Makes insoluble P in soil available to plants |
| *Trichoderma viride/harzianum* | All crops | Suppresses soil-borne fungal diseases; promotes root growth |

**How to apply:** Coat seeds with biofertiliser slurry (200 g per 10 kg seed) before sowing. Store in cool, shaded conditions. Do not mix biofertilisers with chemical fungicides or fertilisers during seed treatment.

## Integrated Nutrient Management (INM) Strategy

For best results, combine organic and chemical inputs:

1. Apply FYM or compost as basal dose (10 t/ha)
2. Reduce chemical N by 25–30% where good quality organic input is used
3. Use biofertiliser seed treatment
4. Apply chemical N as split top-dressings based on crop growth
5. Monitor for micronutrient deficiencies — organic matter improves micronutrient availability

---

## Advisory Note

Organic fertilisers are safe and beneficial, but they require planning ahead (composting takes time; green manuring occupies land). Start with FYM or vermicompost and gradually build toward full INM. Contact your local Krishi Vigyan Kendra (KVK) for locally available organic input sources and state subsidy schemes.
