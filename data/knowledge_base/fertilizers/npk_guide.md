## Introduction

NPK stands for **Nitrogen (N)**, **Phosphorus (P)**, and **Potassium (K)** — the three primary macronutrients that all crops need in significant quantities for healthy growth. Understanding what each nutrient does and when to apply it is fundamental to good farming practice.

**⚠️ Safety Note:** All fertiliser rates mentioned here are general reference values. Actual dose should be based on your soil test results and the specific variety you are growing. Always consult your local Agriculture Extension Officer or Krishi Vigyan Kendra (KVK) before finalising your fertiliser plan.

---

## The Big Three: What N, P, K Do

| Nutrient | Role in Plant | Deficiency Symptom |
|----------|--------------|-------------------|
| **N (Nitrogen)** | Leaf and stem growth; chlorophyll production; protein synthesis | Pale yellow/green leaves starting from older leaves; stunted growth |
| **P (Phosphorus)** | Root development; early plant establishment; flowering and seed formation | Purple/reddish discolouration on undersides of leaves; slow root development |
| **K (Potassium)** | Disease resistance; water regulation; grain and fruit quality; stem strength | Brown scorching at leaf margins; weak stems; poor fruit quality |

## Common Fertiliser Types and Their NPK Content

| Fertiliser | N% | P₂O₅% | K₂O% | Notes |
|-----------|-----|--------|------|-------|
| Urea | 46 | 0 | 0 | Highest nitrogen content; volatile — apply in moist soil |
| DAP (Di-Ammonium Phosphate) | 18 | 46 | 0 | Excellent basal fertiliser for P + N together |
| SSP (Single Super Phosphate) | 0 | 16 | 0 | Also supplies 11% sulphur |
| MOP (Muriate of Potash) | 0 | 0 | 60 | Most common K source |
| SOP (Sulphate of Potash) | 0 | 0 | 50 | Contains 18% sulphur; preferred for chloride-sensitive crops |
| NPK 12:32:16 | 12 | 32 | 16 | Complex fertiliser; easy application |
| NPK 10:26:26 | 10 | 26 | 26 | Higher K; suitable for fruiting crops |

## General Application Principles

### Basal Application (Before/At Sowing)
- Apply the **full dose of P and K** and **one-third to half the N** as a basal dose
- Incorporate into soil before or at the time of sowing
- DAP or SSP is commonly used at this stage

### Top Dressing (After Crop Establishment)
- Apply the **remaining N** in one or two split doses
- First top-dressing: 30–35 days after sowing (at active tillering/vegetative growth)
- Second top-dressing: 50–60 days after sowing (at flowering/panicle initiation) — if required
- Use **urea** for N top-dressing; apply in moist soil to reduce volatilisation

### Why Split Applications?
- Nitrogen is highly mobile in soil and leaches easily
- Split application improves use efficiency and reduces nitrogen loss
- Avoids salt stress from single heavy dose

## Fertiliser Application Tips

1. **Never apply fertiliser to dry soil** — always apply when soil has moisture or before irrigation
2. **Avoid applying on leaves** (broadcast) — causes burning (especially urea); incorporate immediately
3. **Do not mix DAP and urea directly** — apply separately or as compound fertiliser
4. **Store fertilisers in dry, cool conditions** — prevent caking and hygroscopic absorption
5. **Use calibrated equipment** — measure correctly, do not estimate by eye

---

## Advisory Note

Fertiliser recommendations change based on soil health, crop variety, previous crop residue, and irrigation system. A **soil test every 2–3 years** is the most reliable guide. The general doses above are starting reference points only — not prescriptions. Excessive fertiliser use does not increase yield beyond a point and causes soil acidification, groundwater pollution, and crop damage. **Always use fertiliser based on need, not habit.**
