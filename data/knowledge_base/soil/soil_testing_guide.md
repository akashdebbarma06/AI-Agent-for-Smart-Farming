# Soil Testing Guide for Farmers

> **Source:** KrishiMitra AI Knowledge Base — Agricultural Reference Document  
> **Domain:** Soil Information

---

## Why Test Your Soil?

Soil testing removes guesswork from fertiliser decisions. Over-applying fertiliser wastes money and can harm the crop; under-applying limits yield. A soil test tells you:
- The current nutrient status (N, P, K)
- Soil pH (acidity or alkalinity)
- Micronutrient deficiencies (Zinc, Boron, Iron, etc.)
- Organic carbon content

---

## When to Test

- **Best time:** 4–6 weeks before sowing, so you have time to add lime or amendments
- **Frequency:** Every 2–3 years for the same field
- **After major changes:** After flooding, after applying heavy lime, after changing crop rotation

---

## How to Collect a Soil Sample

1. **Divide the field** into uniform sections based on crop history, colour, or texture.
2. **Collect 15–20 sub-samples** per section using a clean spade or auger.
3. **Depth:** 0–15 cm for most crops; 0–30 cm for deep-rooted crops (sugarcane, cotton).
4. Mix all sub-samples from one section into a bucket → take a composite sample of ~500 grams.
5. **Label the sample** clearly: field name, date, crop to be grown.
6. Send to the nearest Soil Testing Laboratory (STL) or Krishi Vigyan Kendra (KVK).

## Understanding Soil pH

| pH Range | Classification | Effect on Crops |
|----------|---------------|-----------------|
| Below 4.5 | Extremely acidic | Aluminium/manganese toxicity; most crops fail |
| 4.5–5.5 | Strongly acidic | Nutrient deficiency; add lime |
| 5.5–6.5 | Mildly acidic | Good for tea, coffee, groundnut |
| 6.5–7.5 | Neutral (optimal) | Best for most food crops |
| 7.5–8.5 | Mildly alkaline | Micronutrient lockup; zinc deficiency common |
| Above 8.5 | Strongly alkaline | Needs gypsum or sulphur treatment |

**To raise pH (reduce acidity):** Apply agricultural lime (calcium carbonate) or dolomite lime.  
**To lower pH (reduce alkalinity):** Apply elemental sulphur or gypsum.

## Understanding NPK Report Values

### Nitrogen (N) — "Builder"
| Status | Available N (kg/ha) | Action |
|--------|--------------------|----|
| Low | < 280 | Apply basal N fertiliser (urea, DAP, or FYM) |
| Medium | 280–560 | Moderate application |
| High | > 560 | Reduce N fertiliser; risk of lodging/pest pressure |

### Phosphorus (P) — "Root Developer"
| Status | Available P (kg/ha) | Action |
|--------|--------------------|----|
| Low | < 10 | Apply DAP or SSP |
| Medium | 10–25 | Moderate P application |
| High | > 25 | Skip additional P; residual P is available |

### Potassium (K) — "Strengthener"
| Status | Available K (kg/ha) | Action |
|--------|--------------------|----|
| Low | < 110 | Apply MOP (muriate of potash) or SOP |
| Medium | 110–280 | Moderate application |
| High | > 280 | No additional K needed |

## Micronutrient Deficiencies — Quick Reference

| Nutrient | Deficiency Symptom | Soil Type at Risk | Remedy |
|----------|--------------------|-------------------|--------|
| Zinc (Zn) | Interveinal chlorosis, stunted growth, bronze discoloration | Alkaline / flooded soils | Zinc sulphate @ 25 kg/ha |
| Boron (B) | Hollow heart in potato/turnip; poor fruit set in sunflower | Sandy, acidic | Borax @ 1–2 kg/ha |
| Iron (Fe) | Yellowing between leaf veins (young leaves) | Calcareous/alkaline | FeSO₄ foliar spray |
| Manganese (Mn) | Interveinal chlorosis (older leaves) | Well-drained alkaline | MnSO₄ foliar spray |
| Sulphur (S) | Uniform yellowing of young leaves | Light sandy soils | Single Super Phosphate or gypsum |

---

## Advisory Note

Always act on soil test results in consultation with your local Agriculture Extension Officer. Fertiliser rates suggested above are general guidelines. District-specific recommendations may differ based on variety, previous crop residues, and local soil history.  
**⚠️ Never apply fertilisers based on guesswork — soil test first.**
