# Tomato Pest and Disease Management Guide

> **Source:** KrishiMitra AI Knowledge Base — Agricultural Reference Document  
> **Domain:** Pest and Disease Management

## Introduction

Tomato is one of the most widely grown vegetables in India, but it is also highly susceptible to a range of pests and diseases. Early identification and timely action — using an Integrated Pest Management (IPM) approach — is critical to protecting yield.

**⚠️ Safety Warning:** Before applying any pesticide, read the product label carefully and follow the recommended dosage. Consult your local Agriculture Extension Officer or agrochemical dealer before using any chemical you have not used before. Never exceed the recommended dose.

## Common Pests

### 1. Fruit Borer (*Helicoverpa armigera*)
- **Symptoms:** Circular holes in fruits; caterpillar found inside fruits; frass (excrement) visible
- **Damage Period:** Fruiting stage
- **IPM Approach:**
  - Install pheromone traps (5 traps per acre) for early detection and mass trapping
  - Hand-pick and destroy larvae during early infestation
  - Spray *Bacillus thuringiensis* (Bt) formulation at first sign — safe and targeted
  - In severe cases, consult label for approved synthetic pyrethroids — **follow dosage strictly**
- **Caution:** Avoid repeated use of same chemical class; rotate modes of action to prevent resistance.

### 2. Whitefly (*Bemisia tabaci*)
- **Symptoms:** Yellowish leaves; sticky honeydew coating; sooty mould on leaves; plants look weak
- **Additional Risk:** Whitefly transmits Tomato Yellow Leaf Curl Virus (TYLCV) — one of the most damaging diseases
- **IPM Approach:**
  - Use yellow sticky traps (10 traps per acre)
  - Remove and destroy infected plant material early
  - Neem oil (0.5–1.0%) spray reduces whitefly populations — safe, residue-free
  - If required, consult agrochemical dealer for approved systemic insecticide — **resist using till threshold is crossed**
- **Threshold:** Act only when > 10 whiteflies per leaf on lower surface.

### 3. Aphids (*Aphis gossypii*)
- **Symptoms:** Clusters of small soft-bodied insects on shoot tips and undersides of young leaves; curling/distortion of leaves
- **IPM Approach:**
  - Strong water jet (syringing) dislodges aphids
  - Neem oil spray (1.0%)
  - Preserve natural enemies (lady beetles, syrphid flies) — avoid broad-spectrum pesticides early
  - Chemical control only if population exceeds threshold; consult dealer for label-approved options

### 4. Leaf Miner (*Liriomyza trifolii*)
- **Symptoms:** Serpentine (winding) white or brown tunnels/mines on leaves
- **IPM Approach:**
  - Remove and destroy heavily mined leaves
  - Yellow sticky traps help reduce adult population
  - Biological control: *Diglyphus isaea* (parasitoid wasp) — naturally present in most fields
  - Chemical options are limited (larva is inside leaf); avoid unnecessary spraying

## Common Diseases

### 1. Early Blight (*Alternaria solani*)
- **Symptoms:** Dark brown target-shaped spots with concentric rings on older leaves; spots have yellow halo; leaves dry up and fall
- **Favourable Conditions:** Warm temperatures (24–29°C), high humidity, alternating wet and dry periods
- **Management:**
  - Remove and destroy infected leaves
  - Avoid overhead irrigation
  - Ensure proper spacing for air circulation
  - Apply copper-based fungicide (e.g. copper oxychloride) or approved mancozeb formulation — **read label for dose and PHI (pre-harvest interval)**
  - Rotate with non-solanaceous crops for 2–3 years

### 2. Late Blight (*Phytophthora infestans*)
- **Symptoms:** Water-soaked olive-green patches on leaves and stems; white mould on undersides of leaves; fruits develop hard, brown lesions; fast-spreading under cool, wet conditions
- **Favourable Conditions:** Cool temperatures (12–18°C), > 85% relative humidity, persistent rain
- **Management:**
  - Plant resistant varieties where available
  - Avoid overhead irrigation
  - Apply approved protectant fungicide (e.g. mancozeb) before disease onset during high-risk weather
  - Systemic fungicide (e.g. metalaxyl + mancozeb) for curative action — **consult dealer for dosage**
  - Remove and destroy heavily infected plants to prevent spread

### 3. Tomato Yellow Leaf Curl Virus (TYLCV)
- **Symptoms:** Yellow curling of leaves; upward leaf curl; stunted plants; pale yellow flowers; no fruit set
- **Cause:** Transmitted by whitefly — control the vector to control the disease
- **Management:**
  - No chemical cure once infected — **remove and destroy infected plants immediately**
  - Use virus-resistant tomato varieties
  - Control whitefly population (see above)
  - Install reflective mulch to repel whiteflies

### 4. Damping Off (*Pythium spp., Rhizoctonia solani*)
- **Symptoms:** Seedling collapse at or near soil level; water-soaked lesion at stem base; most common in nursery beds
- **Management:**
  - Use well-drained nursery media; avoid overwatering
  - Treat soil/media with *Trichoderma viride* bio-fungicide before sowing
  - Seed treatment with thiram or captan — **follow label dose**

## IPM Decision Framework

1. **Monitor first** — Scout field twice a week before taking action
2. **Identify correctly** — Misidentification leads to wrong treatment
3. **Use threshold** — Act only when pest population crosses the economic threshold
4. **Prefer biological and cultural methods** first
5. **Use chemicals as last resort** — Follow dose, dilution, and safety interval (PHI) on label
