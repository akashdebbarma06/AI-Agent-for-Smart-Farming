# Integrated Pest Management (IPM) — General Principles

> **Source:** KrishiMitra AI Knowledge Base — Agricultural Reference Document  
> **Domain:** Pest and Disease Management

---

## What is IPM?

Integrated Pest Management (IPM) is a systematic, ecological approach to managing pests (insects, diseases, weeds) that combines multiple strategies to keep pest damage below economically harmful levels while minimising risk to human health and the environment.

IPM is **not** about eliminating all pests — it is about keeping them below the level where they cause economic damage, using the safest and most targeted methods first.

---

## The Four Pillars of IPM

### 1. Prevention (Cultural Control)
Modify agricultural practices to make the environment less favourable for pests.

- **Crop rotation:** Break the pest cycle by changing crops each season
- **Resistant varieties:** Plant certified disease/pest-resistant varieties when available
- **Timely sowing:** Sowing at the optimal time avoids peak pest pressure seasons
- **Seed treatment:** Treat seeds with bio-agents (*Trichoderma*, *Pseudomonas*) or chemical fungicides before sowing
- **Sanitation:** Remove crop debris, weeds, and volunteer plants that harbour pests between seasons
- **Spacing:** Proper inter-row spacing promotes air circulation, reducing fungal disease risk

### 2. Observation and Monitoring
You cannot manage what you have not measured.

- **Scouting:** Visit the field 2× per week; check leaves (upper and lower surfaces), stems, and soil
- **Pheromone traps:** Use species-specific traps to detect and count adult moth/beetle populations
- **Yellow sticky traps:** Monitor whitefly, aphid, thrip populations
- **Economic Threshold Level (ETL):** Do not spray unless pest population reaches the ETL for that crop-pest combination
  - Example: For rice stem borer — treat when 5% tillers are dead heart during vegetative stage

### 3. Biological Control
Use natural enemies of pests to suppress population growth.

- **Predatory insects:** Lady beetles eat aphids; Chrysopa eats whiteflies; spiders eat many small insects
- **Parasitoids:** *Trichogramma* wasps parasitise eggs of many moth pests; releases at 1 lakh eggs/acre
- **Bio-pesticides:**
  - *Bacillus thuringiensis* (Bt) — kills caterpillars/bollworms; safe for bees, humans, natural enemies
  - *Beauveria bassiana* — entomopathogenic fungus; kills soil-dwelling beetles and whiteflies
  - *Metarhizium anisopliae* — controls locusts, white grubs
  - Neem products — repellent and pest growth disruptor; low mammalian toxicity

### 4. Chemical Control (Last Resort)
Use approved pesticides only when other methods are insufficient and the economic threshold has been crossed.

**Principles for responsible chemical use:**
- **Right product:** Identify the pest correctly before choosing a product
- **Right dose:** Follow label instructions exactly — more is NOT better
- **Right time:** Apply at the most vulnerable stage of the pest's life cycle
- **Right method:** Use appropriate equipment and spray volume
- **Resistance management:** Rotate chemical classes (modes of action) — do not use the same chemistry more than twice per season
- **Safety interval:** Respect the Pre-Harvest Interval (PHI) — the minimum days between last spray and harvest

## Common Mistakes Farmers Make

| Mistake | Why It Causes Problems |
|---------|----------------------|
| Spraying on a fixed calendar, not based on scouting | Wastes money; builds resistance |
| Increasing dose when pest is still present | Residue problems; crop phytotoxicity; resistance |
| Mixing many pesticides without label guidance | Antagonistic reactions; increased toxicity risk |
| Spraying in rain or high wind | Ineffective; drift hazard; water contamination |
| Using same chemical every spray | Rapid resistance development |
| No protective equipment | Health risks |

---

## Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) for Pesticide Application

- Full-sleeved shirt and long pants
- Rubber/chemical-resistant gloves
- Face mask or respirator (for highly toxic chemicals)
- Safety goggles or face shield
- Rubber boots
- **Never eat, drink, or smoke during application**
- Wash hands and face with soap and water immediately after application

## Advisory Note

**⚠️ Critical Safety Reminder:** IPM chemical recommendations are general guidance only. Specific registered products, approved dosages, and state-level restrictions change regularly. Before purchasing or applying any pesticide, consult your local Agriculture Extension Officer, agrochemical dealer, or Krishi Vigyan Kendra (KVK). Always read and follow the full product label.
