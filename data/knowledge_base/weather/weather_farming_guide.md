## Introduction

Weather is the single most variable factor in farming. Understanding how temperature, rainfall, humidity, and wind affect your crops helps you make timely decisions — when to sow, when to irrigate, when to spray, and when to harvest.

This document covers general principles. For your specific field and season, always monitor local weather and consult your local Agriculture Extension Officer.

---

## Temperature and Crops

### How Temperature Affects Growth

- **Too cold:** Slows germination, growth, and fruit set; frost damages tender plants
- **Optimal range:** Each crop has a temperature range where it grows fastest
- **Too hot:** Reduces pollen viability (causes flower drop); increases water stress; speeds up pest/disease cycles

### Crop Temperature Reference

| Crop | Optimal Growth Temp (°C) | Risk Below (°C) | Risk Above (°C) |
|------|--------------------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| Wheat | 15–22 | < 5 (frost damage) | > 30 (grain shrivelling) |
| Paddy | 22–30 | < 15 (chilling injury) | > 35 (sterility) |
| Maize | 21–27 | < 10 (germination failure) | > 32 (pollen damage) |
| Tomato | 20–28 | < 10 (chilling; blossom drop) | > 35 (fruit set failure) |
| Potato | 15–20 | < 0 (frost kills tops) | > 30 (no tuber formation) |
| Cotton | 25–35 | < 15 (germination slow) | > 40 (boll drop) |

## Rainfall and Irrigation

### Key Principles

- **Under-watering (drought stress):** Causes wilting, reduced yield, premature leaf drop
- **Over-watering/waterlogging:** Causes root rot, fungal disease, nutrient leaching, oxygen deprivation
- **Critical growth stages need water most:** Germination, flowering/silking, grain/fruit filling

### Signs Your Crop Needs Water
- Leaf rolling or wilting in early morning (when it is still cool)
- Dry soil 5 cm below surface (test with finger)
- Colour change to dull grey-green

### Signs of Waterlogging
- Standing water in field for > 24 hours
- Yellow lower leaves; poor root development
- Foul smell from waterlogged soil (anaerobic conditions)

## Humidity and Disease Risk

High humidity (> 80%) combined with moderate temperatures creates ideal conditions for most fungal and bacterial diseases.

| Condition | Disease Risk | Caution |
|-----------|-------------|---------|
| > 80% RH + 20–25°C | Very High — Late blight of potato/tomato | Apply protectant fungicide before disease starts |
| > 80% RH + 25–30°C | High — Early blight, anthracnose, powdery mildew | Improve spacing; reduce irrigation frequency |
| Low humidity + hot wind | Risk of spider mites, thrips | Scout more frequently |
| Foggy mornings + cool nights | High rust risk for wheat | Scout for rust spots on leaves |

## Wind Effects on Farming

- **Strong winds (> 40 km/h):** Can cause lodging (falling over) of tall crops like maize and wheat — apply potash fertiliser for stronger stems
- **Hot dry winds (loo winds, summer):** Increase water stress; irrigate before and after loo periods
- **Wind during pesticide spray:** Causes drift; do not spray when wind > 15 km/h; best time is early morning or late evening

## Seasonal Weather Patterns and Farming Decisions

### Kharif Season (June–October)
- Dependent on southwest monsoon
- If monsoon is delayed (> 2 weeks): Consider short-duration drought-tolerant varieties
- If excess rain: Ensure drainage channels are open; do not apply fertilisers in waterlogged fields
- Monitor humidity-driven diseases (blast in rice, downy mildew in soybean)

### Rabi Season (October–March)
- Dependent on residual soil moisture and irrigation
- Watch for frost in January–February in northern India (protect potato, vegetable crops)
- Cold-wave spells: Cover nursery beds; delay transplanting by a few days
- Morning fog in winter: Increases disease pressure (rust, blight)

### Summer Season (March–June)
- High heat stress; water availability critical
- Grow heat-tolerant, short-duration crops only
- Evening irrigation preferred to reduce evaporation
- Monitor for increased pest pressure (whiteflies, mites in hot, dry conditions)

## Practical Weather-Based Decision Guide

| Situation | Recommended Action |
|-----------|-------------------|
| Heavy rain forecast in 24 hours | Delay fertiliser top-dressing (will leach); delay pesticide spray |
| Dry spell (no rain for 7+ days) | Check soil moisture; irrigate if crop shows stress signs |
| Temperature > 40°C for 3+ days | Irrigate daily (light) for vegetables; apply mulch |
| Frost forecast overnight | Cover nursery seedlings and tender transplants with dry straw or plastic |
| High humidity + overcast sky for 3 days | Scout for early disease signs; apply preventive fungicide for susceptible crops |

## Advisory Note

Weather forecasts are available free via the India Meteorological Department (IMD) website and the Meghdoot app for farmers. Use 4-day and 7-day forecasts for planning spray, harvest, and transplanting activities. **This guide provides general principles — local and real-time weather data from reliable sources should always guide your final decision.**
